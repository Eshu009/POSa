Set up once

In order
  1. 1

    Create the database

    Sign up at supabase.com and press New project. Give it a name and a strong database password, and pick the region closest to the shop. It takes about two minutes to finish.

  2. 2

    Create the tables

    In the project, open SQL Editor → New query. Copy everything out of schema.sql, paste it in, press Run. It should say Success.

    Safe to run again later if you ever need to.

  3. 3

    Paste in your two keys

    In Supabase go to Project Settings → API and copy the Project URL and the anon / public key (newer projects call it the publishable key, sb_publishable_… — either works). Open config.js and fill it in:

    export const CONFIG = {
  SUPABASE_URL: 'https://abcdefgh.supabase.co',
  SUPABASE_ANON_KEY: 'eyJhbGciOi....',
};

    The key is meant to be public — it ships inside the page. The SQL you just ran locks every table behind a login, so the key on its own opens nothing.

    But do one more thing: in Supabase go to Authentication → Sign In / Providers → Email and switch off “Allow new users to sign up”. Otherwise a stranger who finds your address can register themselves an account and walk straight into your stock and sales. You create staff logins in the dashboard, so you never need public sign-up.

  4. 4

    Make logins for your staff

    Authentication → Users → Add user. One for you, one for each employee — email and password, tick Auto Confirm User.

    There is no sign-up screen in the app on purpose. Only you can create accounts.

  5. 5

    Put it online

    Open app.netlify.com/drop and drag the whole shopos folder onto the page. No account needed. You get an address like shiny-name-1234.netlify.app.

    It has to be an https address — the camera scanner and the install-as-app option both refuse to work without it.

  6. 6

    Install it on the counter device

    Open the address in Chrome → menu ⋮ → Add to Home screen. You get an icon and a full screen with no browser bars. On a counter PC, Chrome or Edge show an Install icon in the address bar.

  7. 7

    Fill in the shop details

    Sign in, open Setup:

    • Shop name, address, phone — these print on every bill.
    • UPI ID — switches on the pay-by-QR screen. Leave blank to skip it.
    • Tax % — 0 if you are not GST registered. If you are, imitation jewellery is normally 3%; keep prices already include tax ticked when your labelled price is what the customer pays.
    • Receipt width — 58 mm for the common small thermal printer, A5 for a normal one.

Then, every day

Any order

Adding stock

Items → + Item. A barcode number is generated for you — fill in the name, the price and how many pieces came in.

To restock something you already have, open it and use Adjust stock (+12, reason “new lot from supplier”). Every change is logged against whoever made it.

Printing barcode labels

Items → Print labels. It lists your items with a sticker count already filled in from the stock number. Adjust it, choose whether the price prints, press Print. Three labels per row on an A4 sticker sheet.

One barcode is per design, not per piece. Ten identical jhumkas all share code 100001 and you print ten identical stickers. For jewellery a hang tag or a butterfly label around the earring post holds better than sticking onto the piece.

Selling

Three ways to get an item into the cart, all on the Sell screen:

  1. A USB or bluetooth scanner Plug it in and scan. It behaves like a keyboard — types the code, presses enter. Fastest and most reliable by a distance.
  2. The 📷 button Uses the phone camera. Works, but slower, and it struggles with small curved labels.
  3. Type part of the name and tap the item. For when a label is torn.

Then set the quantity, apply a discount (tap ₹ to switch to %), override a rate if you bargained, pick Cash / UPI / Card / Split, press Charge. The bill prints and stock comes down.

With a UPI ID saved, a QR appears first — the customer scans it with any UPI app and you confirm once the money lands. No gateway, no commission.

The cash book

Reports opens on today: bills, gross, discount, net, cash against online, pieces sold. Underneath it, the drawer:

  • Morning — add an Opening cash entry for the float.
  • Through the day — add Expense / cash out for tea, auto, courier, anything taken out.
  • Closing — count the drawer and add a Closing count. The app shows what it expected and whether you are short or in excess.

Returns and backups

Reports → Bills → tap the bill → Return. Stock goes back, the bill is struck through and drops out of the day's totals.

Full JSON backup at the bottom of Reports downloads everything. Once a week into Google Drive. Supabase keeps its own backups; yours costs nothing.

When the internet drops

It keeps billing

Sales queue on the device and a badge in the header counts how many are waiting. They upload on their own once the connection is back, and a queued bill cannot be charged twice — that is enforced inside the database, not by the app.

While offline you can sell but not add new items, and the bill carries a temporary number until it syncs.

One rule: don't sign out while bills are still queued. The app warns you. Wait for the badge to clear.

Later, if you want it

Not needed now

A real .apk file

The installed app already behaves like one. For an actual file to sideload, paste your address into pwabuilder.com and choose Package For Stores → Android → Generate. Keep the package ID as app.netlify.silpapos.twa so it matches the assetlinks.json already in the repo.

It is a shell around this same site, so you never rebuild it — push a change and the installed app picks it up. Two things to get right: paste the fingerprint from the downloaded zip into .well-known/assetlinks.json and push, or the app opens with a browser bar across the top; and keep signing.keystore and its password safe, because losing it means you can never update an already-installed copy.

The online shop

It reads the same product list this counter writes to, so stock stays in one place. It needs two additions — a rule letting the public read products but not write them, and a table for orders. Nothing in the current setup gets in the way.

Supabase's free tier is 500 MB — decades of bills for a shop this size. A free project pauses after a full week of zero activity, which a shop that bills daily never reaches; if you close for a long holiday, open the app once or un-pause it from the dashboard.

Prices are editable at the counter on purpose, and selling below zero stock is allowed with a warning — better a bill that goes through than a customer waiting while you fix a count.
